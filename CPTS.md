# HTB CPTS Review and Experience

## Introduction

The HTB Certified Penetration Testing Specialist (CPTS) ended up being one of the most demanding and rewarding cybersecurity experiences I have completed so far.

Going into the certification, I already had experience with blue team training, SIEM work, threat hunting concepts, and hands-on labs, but CPTS pushed me much deeper into offensive methodology and Active Directory attack path analysis than anything I had done previously.

What stood out immediately was how little the certification relied on memorization alone. Almost everything depended on persistence, structured enumeration, and the ability to connect information across an environment.

A lot of the time, progress came from noticing small details and understanding how different systems, credentials, and permissions related to each other.

---

## Course Material

The CPTS path covered a wide range of topics including:

- Active Directory exploitation
- Web application attacks
- Linux and Windows privilege escalation
- Pivoting and tunneling
- Password attacks
- Internal network enumeration
- Lateral movement
- Reporting and documentation

The Active Directory sections were easily some of the most valuable parts of the training for me personally.

A huge portion of the course focused on understanding relationships between users, groups, systems, permissions, and trust paths rather than simply running automated tools. That forced me to slow down and think more critically during enumeration instead of looking for immediate exploitation opportunities.

The labs also did a good job of reinforcing that smaller findings often become important later in an attack chain.

---

## Lab Experience

The labs felt significantly more realistic than many beginner-level penetration testing environments I had used in the past.

Instead of isolated machines with obvious attack paths, many scenarios required:

- chaining multiple findings together
- pivoting between systems
- reusing credentials intelligently
- analyzing Active Directory permissions
- maintaining organized notes throughout the process

There were a lot of moments where I felt stuck for long periods of time, only to eventually realize that the solution came from something small I had previously overlooked during enumeration.

That experience alone improved my methodology more than simply learning new exploitation techniques.

---

## Exam Experience

The CPTS exam was mentally exhausting in a way that felt very different from traditional certification exams.

The challenge was not simply exploitation difficulty, but maintaining organization, persistence, and clear thinking over a long period of time.

The reporting portion also added a level of realism that I appreciated. Writing detailed findings while actively progressing through the environment forced me to think much more like an actual consultant during an engagement instead of just solving machines.

A huge part of the exam came down to:

- disciplined enumeration
- understanding attack paths
- maintaining documentation
- managing time effectively
- avoiding tunnel vision

The Active Directory portions especially required patience and careful analysis of relationships between accounts, permissions, and systems.

---

## What Stood Out Most

The biggest takeaway for me was how heavily CPTS emphasized methodology over shortcuts.

The certification constantly reinforced that:

- enumeration quality matters
- small details matter
- documentation matters
- attack paths matter
- persistence matters

A lot of progress came from building logical chains between smaller pieces of information instead of finding one major vulnerability immediately.

That made the experience frustrating at times, but also much more rewarding once things started connecting together.

---

## Final Thoughts

Overall, CPTS felt much closer to real offensive security work than many traditional certification experiences.

The certification strengthened my understanding of:

- Active Directory attack paths
- privilege escalation methodology
- lateral movement
- network pivoting
- structured enumeration
- reporting discipline

More than anything, CPTS reinforced the importance of patience, organization, and analytical thinking during offensive operations.

For anyone interested in practical penetration testing methodology and enterprise-style attack path analysis, I think the certification provides an extremely valuable learning experience.
