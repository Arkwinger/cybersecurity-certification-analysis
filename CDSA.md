## Introduction

The HTB Certified Defensive Security Analyst (CDSA) was one of the most practical defensive security training experiences I have completed so far.

Before starting CDSA, I had already completed Blue Team Level 1 (BTL1), which gave me a solid introduction to SIEM workflows, log analysis, threat hunting concepts, and incident response fundamentals. Because of that background, I was already somewhat comfortable navigating investigations and understanding basic defensive workflows going into the certification.

What CDSA did differently was push much deeper into investigation methodology, telemetry analysis, and attacker behavior correlation across enterprise environments.

Coming from both offensive security study and prior blue team training, CDSA helped connect a lot of concepts together from the defender perspective.

---

## Course Material

The CDSA path covered topics including:

- SIEM investigation
- Splunk analysis
- Threat hunting
- Windows event analysis
- Sysmon telemetry
- Incident response workflows
- Log correlation
- MITRE ATT&CK mapping
- Detection engineering concepts

Compared to some defensive training that focuses heavily on theory, CDSA felt much more investigation-driven.

A major strength of the course was how often it required analyzing logs and piecing together attacker behavior step-by-step rather than simply responding to obvious alerts.

The material also reinforced how important visibility and telemetry quality are during investigations.

---

## Lab Experience

The labs focused heavily on investigation methodology and analytical thinking.

A lot of the exercises involved:

- analyzing Windows event logs
- correlating SIEM events
- tracing attacker activity
- identifying suspicious PowerShell usage
- mapping activity to MITRE ATT&CK techniques
- investigating lateral movement behavior

Because I had already completed BTL1 beforehand, I noticed that CDSA felt more advanced in terms of telemetry depth and investigation complexity.

BTL1 gave me a strong foundation in defensive concepts, while CDSA pushed further into enterprise-style investigation workflows and larger-scale event analysis.

The Splunk-focused portions were especially valuable because they required thinking critically about how attacker activity actually appears from the defensive side of the environment.

---

## Exam Experience

The CDSA exam felt very different from offensive security certifications.

Instead of exploitation and privilege escalation, the challenge came from:

- investigation accuracy
- log interpretation
- evidence correlation
- identifying attacker behavior
- maintaining attention to detail
- distinguishing malicious activity from normal noise

The exam rewarded patience and structured thinking much more than speed.

A large portion of the difficulty came from connecting events together logically and understanding how attacker actions create patterns across logs and telemetry sources.

Compared to offensive exams, the experience felt much more analytical and investigation-focused throughout.

---

## What Stood Out Most

One of the biggest takeaways for me was how much offensive activity becomes visible once proper telemetry and logging are configured.

The certification reinforced the importance of:

- Sysmon visibility
- process creation logging
- PowerShell monitoring
- network telemetry
- SIEM correlation
- behavioral analysis

Coming from offensive security training as well, it was interesting recognizing many attacker techniques from the opposite side of the environment.

CDSA helped bridge the gap between offensive tradecraft and defensive visibility in a way that made both skill sets stronger together.

---

## Final Thoughts

Overall, CDSA provided a very strong defensive security learning experience focused on realistic investigation workflows and attacker behavior analysis.

Combined with previous blue team training like BTL1, the certification helped deepen my understanding of:

- threat hunting
- SIEM operations
- Windows telemetry
- attacker behavior analysis
- incident response methodology
- defensive investigation workflows

For me personally, CDSA paired extremely well with offensive security study because it provided a much clearer understanding of how attacker activity appears from the defender perspective inside enterprise environments.
